You walk into the faculty of law law library at 4:00 PM. 

Across every long mahogany table sit dozens of undergraduates buried behind stacks of red, blue, and black leather-bound tomes: the *Nigerian Weekly Law Reports (NWLR)*, *All Nigeria Law Reports (All NLR)*, Sagay’s *Nigerian Law of Contract*, Kodilinye’s *Law of Torts*, and the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria.

Coursemates are muttering Latin phrases under their breath: *nemo dat quod non habet*, *audi alteram partem*, *res ipsa loquitur*.

Your Contract Law lecturer, an accomplished Senior Advocate of Nigeria (SAN), announced the semester examination format earlier that morning:

> *"Your exam paper will consist of four compulsory hypothetical problem scenarios. I do not want storytelling. Every legal assertion you make must be supported by judicial authority or statutory provisions. If you cite no cases, you fail."*

In Nigerian Faculties of Law accredited by the Council of Legal Education (CLE) and the [National Universities Commission (NUC)](https://www.nuc.edu.ng/), the sheer volume of reading is staggering. An undergraduate enrolled in 300 Level must master **over 120 judicial precedents per semester** across Criminal Law, Tort, Land Law, Commercial Law, and Constitutional Law.

Many law students study by re-reading 500 pages of judgment transcripts over and over. By the third week of revision, their brain is a chaotic blur of party names, dissenting opinions, and confusing dates. When handed an exam script, they commit the fatal mistake of **"Case Dumping"**—listing fifteen case names without stating the underlying legal principles.

Law exams do not test raw memorization; they test **legal application**. 

Here is the exact **3-Part Case Card System** and the **IRAC framework** used by First Class law scholars to memorize hundreds of authorities effortlessly and write top-tier examination scripts.

---

## 1. The "Case Dumping" Trap: Why Listing Cases Earns Zero Marks

The most common error in law faculties is believing that the student who cites the most cases wins the highest grade.

Imagine a contract problem question involving an advertisement of a reward for lost property. 

* **The Failing "Case Dumping" Approach:**
  > *"In this case, the seller made an offer. This is supported by Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co, Fisher v Bell, Pharmaceutical Society v Boots, and Partridge v Crittenden. Therefore, the buyer wins."*

An experienced law professor will award this answer **3 out of 15 marks**. Why? Because the student merely dumped four case names without explaining what rules they established or how they apply to the prompt.

* **The Winning Legal Application:**
  > *"The primary legal issue is whether the display constitutes an offer capable of acceptance or a mere invitation to treat. As established in the landmark English decision in Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co [1893], a unilateral offer made to the whole world becomes binding upon performance of the stated conditions, distinct from a general invitation to treat as seen in Fisher v Bell [1961]..."*

Examiners award marks for the **Ratio Decidendi** (the underlying legal principle of the decision), not the font size of the case title.

## 2. The 3-Part "Case Card" Architecture

Human memory stores vivid narratives, visual cues, and structural connections far better than dry Latin lists. 

Stop writing 10-page case summaries. For every landmark precedent, create a **3-Part Case Card**:



| Framework Component | Structural Element | Practical Exam Application |
|---|---|---|
| **Part 1: Identity Anchor** | Case Names & Law Report Year | E.g., *Donoghue v Stevenson [1932] AC 562* — establishes legal authority |
| **Part 2: Visual Hook** | Vivid, Bizarre Material Fact | E.g., Decomposed snail inside an opaque ginger beer bottle — triggers instant neural recall |
| **Part 3: Ratio Decidendi** | Core Legal Principle | E.g., The "Neighbor Principle" establishing the modern law of negligence — scores core marks |



### Example Case Card 1: The Law of Torts (Negligence)
1. **Case Anchor:** *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562
2. **Visual Hook:** *The Decomposed Snail in the Opaque Ginger Beer Bottle.* (A woman drinks ginger beer bought by a friend; a rotting snail pours out of the dark glass, causing severe gastroenteritis).
3. **Ratio Decidendi:** Established the **"Neighbor Principle"** in negligence: A manufacturer owes a legal duty of care to the ultimate consumer to prevent injury, even in the absence of a direct contractual relationship.

### Example Case Card 2: Nigerian Constitutional Law
1. **Case Anchor:** *Awolowo v Federal Electoral Commission & Shagari* [1979] 6-9 SC 51
2. **Visual Hook:** *The Two-Thirds Fraction of 19 States.* (An electoral dispute over whether thirteen states meant 12 states plus two-thirds of the thirteenth state).
3. **Ratio Decidendi:** The Supreme Court adopted the **Literal Rule of Statutory Interpretation**, holding that two-thirds of nineteen states was twelve and two-thirds states, not thirteen whole states.

### Example Case Card 3: Nigerian Property & Land Law
1. **Case Anchor:** *Savannah Bank of Nigeria Ltd v Ajilo* [1989] 1 NWLR (Pt. 97) 305
2. **Visual Hook:** *The Mortgage Without Governor's Consent.* (A mortgagor attempted to invalidate his own mortgage deed because the Governor never consented).
3. **Ratio Decidendi:** Any alienation or mortgage of a Right of Occupancy under the **Land Use Act of 1978** without the prior consent of the State Military Governor is null, void, and of no legal effect.

When you associate every case with an absurd or distinct material fact (the snail, the smoke ball, the two-thirds fraction), your brain recalls the judicial name instantly.

## 3. The IRAC Exam Script Formula: Writing Like a Judge

When you open your exam question booklet, organize every problem answer using the universal **IRAC Framework**:



* I - Issue] ➔ [R - Rule & Precedent] ➔ [A - Application & Analysis] ➔ [C - Conclusion



### The 4 Steps in Action:

| IRAC Element | Operational Definition | Sample Sentence Starter |
|---|---|---|
| **I - Issue (15%)** | State the precise legal question raised by the hypothetical facts | *"The core issue for determination is whether..."* |
| **R - Rule (35%)** | Quote the relevant statutory section and judicial authority (*Ratio*) | *"Under Section 15 of the Act, and as decided in [Case Name]..."* |
| **A - Application (40%)** | Compare the scenario facts with the precedent facts (Analogize or Distinguish) | *"Applying the principle to the instant facts, Mr. K acted in manner identical to..."* |
| **C - Conclusion (10%)** | Deliver a definitive legal outcome or advise the client on liability | *"In conclusion, it is submitted that the plaintiff will succeed in an action for..."* |

### The Power of "Distinguishing" Cases
A student who simply applies a rule gets a 'B'. A student who **distinguishes** a case gets an 'A':
> *"While the defendant may rely on the defense of necessity as raised in R v Dudley and Stephens, that authority must be distinguished from the present facts because here, no immediate peril to human life existed."*

Distinguishing proves to the professor that you understand the precise boundaries of legal doctrine.

## 4. The 2-Page "Master Case Matrix" for Exam Week

In the final 48 hours before an exam, you cannot flip through 400 pages of notes. 

Condense your entire syllabus into a **2-Page Master Case Matrix**:

| Topic | Primary Statute | Landmark Precedent | The Core Ratio (10 Words or Less) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Offer vs Invitation to Treat** | Sale of Goods Act | *Fisher v Bell* [1961] | Display of goods with price tag is a mere invitation |
| **Acceptance by Post** | Common Law Rule | *Adams v Lindsell* [1818] | Acceptance complete the moment letter is posted |
| **Corporate Personality** | CAMA 2020, Sec 42 | *Salomon v Salomon* [1897] | Company has separate legal personality from its shareholders |
| **Ultra Vires Doctrine** | CAMA 2020 | *Ashbury Carriage Co v Riche* | Acts beyond memorandum scope are void at common law |
| **Executive Immunity** | 1999 Constitution, Sec 308 | *Tinubu v I.M.B.* [2001] | No civil or criminal proceedings against sitting governor |

Reviewing this matrix three times a day during exam week burns the authorities into your visual memory, enabling rapid script production.

## The Law Student's Daily Study Checklist

Execute this checklist for every legal course this session:

- [ ] **Build 3-part Case Cards:** Capture Name, Visual Hook, and Ratio Decidendi for every new decision.
- [ ] **Answer one problem question weekly using IRAC:** Practice isolating the issue and applying the rule.
- [ ] **Never memorize dates over principles:** Focus on the court's reasoning; citation years are secondary.
- [ ] **Practice distinguishing similar cases:** Know the exact factual differences that change the verdict.
- [ ] **Compress notes into a 2-page Master Matrix:** Maintain an active summary of statutory sections and key precedents.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do examiners grade case law citations in Nigerian law faculties?
Law examiners do not award marks for simply listing case names. Under Council of Legal Education standards, marks are awarded for identifying the material legal issue, citing the correct case, stating the court's exact ratio decidendi (legal principle), and applying it to the scenario facts using the IRAC method.

### What is the IRAC method for answering law exam problem questions?
IRAC stands for Issue, Rule, Application, and Conclusion. In an exam answer, you first identify the legal problem (Issue), state the governing statute and judicial precedent (Rule), analyze how the facts match or differ from the precedent (Application), and provide a definitive legal resolution (Conclusion).

### Do you need to memorize exact Nigerian Weekly Law Report (NWLR) citations in exams?
No. While including exact citations (e.g., [1989] 1 NWLR (Pt. 97) 305) demonstrates elite mastery, university examiners do not deduct marks if you omit the volume and page number, provided you state the correct party names, the decade, and the accurate legal principle.

### How many cases should a law student know per course?
A solid LL.B revision matrix requires between 25 and 40 landmark authorities per course. Trying to memorize 150 obscure cases causes cognitive overload; focus on the foundational decisions that established core legal doctrines and their modern statutory exceptions.
