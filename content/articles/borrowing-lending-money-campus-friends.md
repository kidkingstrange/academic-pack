# Borrowing Money on Campus: Agreements That Protect Trust

Your closest coursemate walks into your room on a Tuesday evening.

His face is strained. He closes the door quietly, sits on your plastic chair, and leans forward:

*"Bros, abeg, I am in a serious mess. My lodge landlord gave me 48 hours to pay my balance or he will lock my room. Please, lend me ₦25,000. I swear on my life, my uncle is sending my allowance on Friday. I will transfer it back to you on Friday morning without fail."*

You have ₦30,000 in your account—your entire feeding and textbook budget for the next four weeks.

He is your friend. You study together. You share past questions. 

You transfer the ₦25,000.

Friday arrives. No transfer. 

Saturday passes. Silence. 

On Monday, you ask him about the money. He avoids eye contact, speaks irritably, and says: *"Ah, bros, why are you rushing me? My uncle has not sent it now. Have small patience! You know I cannot run away with your money."*

Two weeks later, the friendship has disintegrated into cold glares, avoided lecture seats, and simmering hostility.

Informal campus lending is a minefield that destroys friendships, fuels physical hostel brawls, and leaves lenders financially stranded.

Here is the objective framework to handle borrowing and lending on campus with absolute integrity and mutual protection.

---

## 1. The Fundamental Law of Campus Lending

> **The Golden Law:** Never lend money to a campus friend that you cannot afford to write off as a permanent gift.

| Audit Question | Scenario A: High Risk | Scenario B: Managed Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| **"If they default 100%, will I starve, fall behind on rent, or fail an exam?"** | **YES (Capital is critical)** | **NO (Discretionary cushion)** |
| **Recommended Decision** | **Decline Firmly** | **Lend with Written Terms** |
| **Action Script** | *"I sympathize with your situation, but 100% of my funds are locked for non-negotiable school fees and rent this week."* | Execute a simple written agreement with a fixed repayment date and verified source of funds before transferring a kobo. |

If losing that money will compromise your semester feeding, exam docket clearance, or health, **lending it is reckless self-sabotage.**

## 2. The Written Micro-Contract Protocol

If you choose to lend money to a coursemate, remove ambiguity by creating a simple written **WhatsApp Loan Memorandum**:

> ### Loan Confirmation Memo
>
> * Date: 26/09/2026
> * Lender: Itoya David
> * Borrower: [Friend's Name]
> * Principal Amount: ₦15,000 (Transferred via OPay)
> * Repayment Date: Strictly on or before Friday, 10/10/2026
> * Repayment Source: October Family Allowance Remittance
> * Agreement: Both parties agree this is a formal debt, not a gift.
> * **Borrower replies: "Confirmed and agreed."**

A written confirmation in an active WhatsApp chat:
* Eliminates the psychological drift where borrowers conveniently "forget" the exact sum.
* Establishes a concrete calendar repayment deadline.
* Provides undisputed written evidence if escalation to mutual friends becomes necessary.

## 3. How to Decline Loan Requests with Grace

Saying "no" to a friend feels uncomfortable, but bad loans destroy friendships far faster than clean boundaries:

### Script A: The "Pre-Allocated Capital" Defense
> *"Emeka, I value our friendship deeply and I understand how tight things are. However, my remaining balance is pre-allocated for my final semester departmental project binding this week. I don't have surplus liquid funds to lend."*

### Script B: The "Partial Gift Alternative"
> *"Chinedu, I cannot lend you ₦20,000 because I cannot risk my feeding budget. But because you are my brother, take this ₦3,000 as a free gift to sort out food for the next few days. You don't need to pay it back."*

Giving a small ₦2,000 or ₦3,000 gift protects your core capital, helps your friend immediately, and carries zero toxic debt overhang.

## 4. Protect Your Integrity as a Borrower

If you are the one borrowing:
* Treat repayment deadlines like sacred oaths.
* If your expected transfer is delayed, **reach out to the lender before they ask you**:
  > *"Bros, my family's transfer was delayed until Monday. I want to keep you informed. You will receive your funds the moment it drops."*
* Proactive communication preserves trust, protects your reputation, and ensures you will always find a helping hand in seasons of true need.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do informal loans between university coursemates usually destroy friendships?
Lack of clear written repayment deadlines: borrowers treat informal loans as indefinite gifts, while lenders expect immediate repayment, breeding silent resentment and bitter confrontation.

### What should a simple student written loan agreement contain?
Principal amount borrowed, specific calendar repayment date, clear method of repayment (bank transfer), and the signatures of both parties and one neutral eyewitness.

### What is the 'Golden Rule' of lending money to campus coursemates?
Never lend an amount you cannot afford to write off as a complete loss. If lending ₦10,000 means you will starve if they default, politely decline or gift them ₦2,000 instead.

### How can you decline a friend's loan request without causing offense?
Use the 'Committed Capital' decline script: 'I would love to help, but my remaining funds are strictly allocated for my faculty registration dues this Friday. I cannot lend money right now.'
