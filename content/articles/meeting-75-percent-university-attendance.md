Across almost every Nigerian public and private university—from UNILAG and OAU to Covenant and FUTO—a strict regulation hangs over student dockets:

> *"Students must achieve a minimum of 75% physical lecture attendance to be eligible to sit for the end-of-semester examination."*

For full-time students with zero financial responsibilities, hitting 75% is simply a matter of waking up in time.

For working students, however, it is a high-wire balancing act. You might be managing a physical 9:00 AM to 2:00 PM shift at a campus tech hub, troubleshooting client server issues, or running a POS kiosk that pays your accommodation fees.

Miss one too many classes, and you arrive at the exam hall only to find your matriculation number struck off the official examination register.

Here is the operational blueprint for hitting 75% lecture attendance systematically while working a job.

---

## The Mathematics of the 75% Attendance Threshold

Understanding the arithmetic behind university attendance prevents accidental disqualification.

A typical 3-unit course runs for 14 active teaching weeks at 3 lecture hours per week, totaling approximately 14 to 15 lecture sessions:

> ### The Attendance Margin Of Error
>
> * Total Course Lecture Sessions: 14 Minimum Required for 75%: 11 Sessions Maximum Allowable Absences: 3 Sessions

Missing just **four lectures** across an entire semester instantly drops your attendance to 71.4%, making you legally ineligible to sit for the examination.

## 3 Strategies for Working Students to Protect Attendance

### 1. Front-Load Attendance During Weeks 1 Through 6
Many students skip classes during the first month of the semester because *"lecturers are just introducing the course."* 

This is a disastrous strategy for working students. By attending 100% of lectures in the first 6 weeks:
* You accumulate an immediate attendance credit buffer.
* When client emergencies, project crunches, or sudden shift conflicts occur in Weeks 9 through 12, you can safely miss two classes without breaching the 75% line.

### 2. Identify the 'High-Risk' Lecturers Early
Not all lecturers take attendance with identical rigor. Categorize your department's lecturers by Week 2:
* **Strict Gatekeepers:** Lecturers who call the register personally at 8:05 AM and lock classroom doors. *Rule: You must attend 100% of these classes in person.*
* **Continuous Assessment Integrated:** Lecturers who give random 5-minute unannounced quizzes at the end of class. *Rule: Inviolable physical attendance.*
* **Informal Roll Calls:** Large faculty-wide GST classes with 800 students where attendance is taken irregularly. *Rule: Monitor via class representatives.*

To make the most of short breaks between mandatory physical lectures, explore our framework on [micro-studying in 15-minute campus gaps](/blog/balancing-work-and-school/micro-studying-between-university-classes/).

### 3. The 'Class Rep' Intelligence Channel
Your Departmental Class Representative is the operational hub of academic scheduling. 

Cultivate a supportive relationship with your class rep:
* Offer genuine value (e.g., share your concise digital study notes or assist with course group logistics).
* Class reps know in advance when a lecturer has rescheduled an 8:00 AM class to 3:00 PM, allowing you to rearrange client shifts with hours to spare rather than standing outside an empty hall.

---

## Why You Must Avoid 'Proxy Attendance' (Signing for Friends)

When pressure mounts, many working students ask friends to write their name on the attendance register.

This is an extremely hazardous gamble:
1. **Headcount Audits:** Strict lecturers frequently count the physical heads in the room and compare against the total signatures on the sheet.
2. **Disciplinary Panel Referral:** If your name appears on a sheet when you were not physically present, it constitutes official documentary fraud under university statutes.
3. **The Risk-to-Reward Ratio:** Risking a 1-year suspension to save a 45-minute lecture is irrational.

If you must miss a class due to an unavoidable work emergency, submit a formal written medical or family notification to the departmental office through legitimate channels.

## Action Checklist: 75% Attendance Defense

- [ ] Audit every course on your registration docket and calculate the maximum allowable misses.
- [ ] Maintain an active attendance tracker on your phone or notebook.
- [ ] Attend 100% of classes during Weeks 1 to 6 to build a safety reserve.
- [ ] Reconfigure client working hours to evenings (after 4:30 PM) to avoid daytime lecture collisions.
- [ ] Never engage in proxy signing or impersonation on attendance registers.
- [ ] Immediately review course materials for any class you are forced to miss.
