# Black Tax on Students: Managing Extended Family Demands

You sit in your hostel room.

You just secured a small remote freelance graphic design gig or saved ₦15,000 from weeks of skipping canteen lunches to buy a refurbished Android phone for your project research.

Your phone buzzes.

It is an extended relative—an uncle or aunt who sent you ₦5,000 during your 100-level matriculation two years ago.

The voice message begins warmly, then shifts: *"My child, how are your studies? God will bless you. Listen, your cousin in the village is sick and needs ₦12,000 for hospital treatment. We know you are in the university now and you are doing big things. Please send whatever you have to this account immediately."*

Your chest clenches in familiar panic.

If you explain that you are a broke undergraduate surviving on garri and beans, they will accuse you of being stingy, proud, or ungrateful: *"So because you are in university, you have forgotten your family?"*

If you transfer the money, your bank account drops to zero, and you will spend the next three weeks starving during Continuous Assessment tests.

This is the hidden financial tax on ambitious Nigerian youths: **Undergraduate Black Tax**.

Here is how to set loving, unshakeable boundaries without destroying family relationships.

---

## 1. The Myth of the "Wealthy University Student"

In many rural Nigerian communities, attending tertiary education is culturally equated with financial wealth:

> ### Relative'S Perception
>
> * University student = Living in luxury in the big city
> * Has access to scholarships, politicians, and wealthy friends
> * Ought to contribute to weddings, burials, and sibling emergencies
> * **The Student Reality**
> * Skipping meals to print lecture handouts
> * Surviving in a 6-man room with erratic power and no running water
> * Bank balance: ₦1,250

When relatives demand financial bailouts, they are not acting out of malice; they are operating under a profound delusion regarding the economic reality of modern Nigerian university life.

## 2. The Trap of Financial Secrecy vs. Financial Vulnerability

The biggest mistake students make when they start earning small income (freelancing, campus hair-dressing, tech gigs) is **Status Signaling**:
* Posting photos in restaurants on WhatsApp status.
* Buying fashionable clothes to look successful on campus.
* Announcing online earnings to cousins.

The moment extended relatives see signs of prosperity, you become the designated family safety net.

| Public Stance | Consequence | Safe Behavioral Standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Flashy WhatsApp status posts** | Immediate influx of emergency loan requests | Complete privacy regarding income and savings |
| **Defensive angry arguments** | Accusations of arrogance and family betrayal | Calm, respectful, and brief boundary scripts |
| **Sacrificing exam fees for family** | Academic failure, carryovers, drop out | Non-negotiable protection of education budget |

## 3. The 3 Golden Boundary Scripts

You never need to lie. You simply state the reality of your student situation with calm respect:

### Script 1: For Demands from Extended Uncles/Aunts
> *"Good afternoon Uncle. Thank you for checking on my studies. I am very sorry to hear about the situation with cousin. Unfortunately, as a student living strictly on an allowance that barely covers my feeding and exam materials, I do not have personal savings or funds to assist with this right now. I am praying that things resolve quickly."*

### Script 2: For Sibling Financial Requests
> *"Sister, I love you, but right now on campus, my remaining balance is for my faculty exam clearances this month. I cannot send money now without putting my exams in jeopardy. Let me focus on finishing my degree so I can support you properly once I start working."*

## 4. Your First Obligation Is Graduation

Remember this foundational truth:
* You cannot pull someone out of a ditch if you jump into the ditch with them.
* If you drain your student funds, get sick, fail your exams, or drop out of school, you become another financial burden on your family.
* Your greatest long-term service to your entire family lineage is to graduate with high academic honors, acquire lucrative skills, and establish your own financial independence.

Protect your education with absolute conviction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is 'Student Black Tax' in Nigeria?
The financial expectations placed on university undergraduates by extended family members, relatives, or parents who assume university students have surplus cash or should solve family emergencies.

### How can a student handle uncles or aunts asking for loans or contributions?
Be transparent about your non-earning student status: 'Uncle, I am currently living on a tight monthly student feeding allowance and do not have commercial income. I wish I could assist, but I cannot right now.'

### Is it wrong to refuse to send money home when you need it for exams and feeding?
No. Sacrificing your nutrition, health, or examination fees to fund family emergencies will only cause you to fail, ensuring that the cycle of financial dependency continues.

### How should a student handle relatives who claim 'we paid your school fees' to demand money?
Express deep gratitude for their past support while firmly maintaining that your primary duty right now is graduating with high honors so you can build a stable career to support the family properly.
