# Dropping an Elective vs Risking a D: Credit Unit Maths

It is Week 5 of the semester. 

Your Course Level Adviser approved your registration form three weeks ago. To boost your academic profile, you registered **24 maximum credit units**—including an optional 2-unit elective in Advanced Econometrics, French for Beginners, or Introduction to Astronomy.

Now, the mid-semester Continuous Assessment tests are underway. 

You are drowning. 

Your 4-unit departmental core courses demand twenty hours of weekly problem-solving, your laboratory reports take eight hours every Wednesday, and this "simple" 2-unit elective has turned into a nightmare: an eccentric lecturer who demands a 40-page term paper, unannounced quizzes, and complex formulas.

You sit at your desk with your scientific calculator, faced with a high-stakes strategic question:

> *"Should I file an Add/Drop form to drop this elective right now, or should I push through and risk getting a 'D' or 'E'?"*

In the Nigerian university system operating on the [National Universities Commission (NUC)](https://www.nuc.edu.ng/) 5.0 Cumulative Grade Point Average scale, **academic progression is an optimization problem.**

Every credit unit you register adds to your cumulative denominator. Registering unnecessary units that yield low letter grades permanently deflates your graduating degree class.

Here is the exact mathematical model to decide whether to drop an elective or fight for a pass, and how to protect your CGPA from unnecessary damage.

---

## 1. The Denominator Trap: How Low Grades Silently Destroy CGPA

To understand the mathematical risk of an elective, examine how the NUC 5.0 CGPA formula works:



> 💡 **Key Formula:** *CGPA = (Total Cumulative Grade Points) / (Total Cumulative Credit Units Registered)*



| Letter Grade | Percentage Score | NUC Grade Point Value | 2-Unit Course Value | 3-Unit Course Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 70% – 100% | 5 Points | 10 Points | 15 Points |
| **B** | 60% – 69% | 4 Points | 8 Points | 12 Points |
| **C** | 50% – 59% | 3 Points | 6 Points | 9 Points |
| **D** | 45% – 49% | 2 Points | 4 Points | 6 Points |
| **E** | 40% – 44% | 1 Point | 2 Points | 3 Points |
| **F** | Below 40% | 0 Points | 0 Points | 0 Points |

### The Mathematical Shock:
Imagine a student with a solid **3.60 CGPA** across 60 completed units (216 Total Points).

In the current semester, they register an optional 2-unit elective. They are overloaded, neglect the course, and score a **46% (D)**:
* New Points Added: $2 	imes 2 = 4	ext{ Points}$
* New Units Added: $2	ext{ Units}$
* New Cumulative Standing: $(216 + 4) / (60 + 2) = 220 / 62 = \mathbf{3.54	ext{ CGPA}}$

That single elective cost the student **0.06 CGPA points**, bringing them within inches of slipping out of Second Class Upper into Second Class Lower.

## 2. The 3 Questions That Decide: Drop or Push?

Before the university Senate's **Add/Drop deadline** passes, ask yourself these three analytical questions:



* 1. Is this course a statutory requirement for graduation?
* 2. Is the predicted grade below a 'C' (3.0)?
* 3. Does this course steal cognitive time from 4-unit core papers?



### Question 1: Is this course a compulsory prerequisite?
Check your faculty handbook. If the course is a **Departmental Elective Option** (e.g., you need any 2 elective units from a basket of 4 options), you do not have to take *this* specific difficult course. You can drop it and take a lighter option next semester.

### Question 2: What is your realistic grade ceiling?
Be brutally honest with your Continuous Assessment score:
* If you scored **8/30** on the CA test, you need to score an incredible **62/70** on the final written paper just to reach an 'A' (70%), or **42/70** just to scrape a 'C' (50%).
* If your ceiling is a 'D' or 'E', **drop the course immediately**.

### Question 3: Is it draining your 4-unit core prerequisites?
Failing or getting a 'D' in a 4-unit core prerequisite destroys your academic standing four times faster than an elective. If spending 10 hours on this elective causes you to get a 'C' instead of an 'A' in a 4-unit core paper, dropping the elective is a mathematical no-brainer.

## 3. The Official Add/Drop Protocol: Avoiding the "Phantom F"

Never stop attending an elective course without formally dropping it on your portal.

If you simply walk away from a registered course:
* The departmental broad-sheet will record your score as **0 / 100 (ABS)**.
* Senate software automatically converts an unexplained absence into an **'F' (0 points)**.
* That avoidable 'F' adds zero points to your numerator while inflating your denominator by 2 or 3 units, dragging down your GPA.

### The 3-Step Add/Drop Procedure:
1. **Collect the Official Add/Drop Form:** Obtain the form from your Faculty Academic Officer before the statutory 4-to-6-week deadline.
2. **Secure Signatures:** Have your Course Level Adviser and Head of Department sign the deletion.
3. **Verify Portal Update:** Log into your student portal and ensure the course code is completely deleted from your registered course list.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you drop an elective course after registering it in a Nigerian university?
Yes, provided you submit an official 'Add and Drop Form' within the university Senate's approved window (typically within the first 4 to 6 weeks of the semester). Dropping a course outside this window records an automatic 'ABS' or 'F'.

### How does a 'D' grade in a 2-unit elective affect your CGPA?
On the 5.0 NUC scale, a 'D' is worth 2 points per unit (4 points total for a 2-unit course). While it avoids a carryover, it pulls your semester GPA downward, requiring higher grades in core courses to maintain a 3.50+ average.

### What happens if you fail an elective course in Nigeria?
If the course is an optional elective (not a compulsory departmental requirement), university Senate regulations allow you to either retake it or replace it with an alternative approved elective to fulfill total graduation credit units.

### How many total credit units are required to graduate in Nigerian universities?
Under NUC CCMAS/BMAS guidelines, a standard 4-year undergraduate degree requires between 120 and 150 completed credit units, while 5-year engineering and law programs require 150 to 180 credit units.
