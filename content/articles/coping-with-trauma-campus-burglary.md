# Trauma Recovery After Campus Robbery or Attack

It is 2:30 AM in your off-campus lodge.

Suddenly, you hear the violent splintering of a wooden door down the corridor. Loud, aggressive voices shout: *"Open the door or we will shoot! Bring out your phones and laptops!"*

For the next forty-five minutes, your compound is terrorized. Your door is kicked in. Flashlights shine directly into your eyes. You lie face down on the cold floor, heart hammering against your ribs, while your phone, your laptop containing four years of project files, your generator, and every naira in your room are carted away.

The robbers leave into the darkness.

The next morning, the police arrive, write statements, and depart. Your landlord promises to "look into the compound gate." Your parents send condolences and prayers.

Two weeks later, the physical chaos settles, but your mind is in pieces:
* Every time a motorcycle accelerates outside your window at night, your chest clenches in terror.
* You cannot sleep without a bright light on.
* You cannot concentrate on lecture notes for five minutes without picturing the weapon pointed at your face.
* And your semester examinations begin in eighteen days.

This is **Acute Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)**. Here is how to navigate the neurological and psychological aftermath of a campus attack and recover your academic stability.

---

## 1. The Neurobiology of Acute Campus Trauma

During a violent robbery or assault, your brain's amygdala triggers a catastrophic surge of **epinephrine and cortisol**:

* **Hypervigilance:** Your nervous system gets locked in "Threat Mode." The brain treats ordinary sounds—a roommate closing an iron gate, footsteps in the dark—as life-threatening predators.
* **Intrusive Flashbacks:** The traumatic memory is stored in the hippocampus without normal chronological time tags. When triggered, your body experiences the robbery as if it is happening right now in real time.
* **Emotional Detachment & Dissociation:** You may feel numb, distant from friends, and completely indifferent to your studies, as if academic grades no longer matter.

## 2. Immediate Tactical Safety and Academic Protections

Do not attempt to "tough it out" alone in a compromised room. Take these four protective steps immediately:

### Step 1: Relocate Your Physical Sleeping Space
If your door lock is broken or the compound has no perimeter security, do not sleep there. Pack essential clothes and temporarily move in with a trusted friend in a secure, guarded lodge or campus hostel. Physical safety is the non-negotiable prerequisite for nervous system regulation.

Review comprehensive lodge safety protocols in [off-campus safety for students in Nigerian university towns](/blog/hostel/off-campus-safety-female-students-nigeria/).

### Step 2: File Official Documentation for Academic Leniency
Do not wait until after you fail an exam to explain that you were robbed. Within 48 hours of the incident:
1. File an official police report at the nearest divisional station (keep two signed photocopies).
2. Visit the University Health Center and Guidance Counseling Unit to document acute stress symptoms.
3. Submit a formal letter to your Departmental HOD and Faculty Dean requesting test rescheduling or assignment grace periods, attaching your police and medical reports.

## 3. Somatic Protocols to Settle a Traumatized Nervous System

When panic attacks strike while you are trying to study:

| Somatic Intervention | Execution Method | Clinical Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **The 5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Grounding** | Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste | Pulls prefrontal cortex out of flashback loop into present reality |
| **Bilateral Tactile Stimulation** | Cross arms over chest and alternate tapping left and right shoulders slowly | Mimics EMDR therapy; stimulates interhemispheric communication |
| **Extended Exhalation Breathing** | Inhale deeply through nose for 4 seconds; exhale slowly through mouth for 7 seconds | Directly stimulates vagus nerve to lower elevated heart rate |

## 4. Rebuilding Academic Momentum from Zero

Losing your laptop and lecture notes feels like having your academic foundation wiped out:
* **The Cloud Backup Imperative:** From this day forward, never store lecture notes or project drafts solely on a physical device. Save drafts to Google Drive, Telegram saved messages, or Notion after every study session.
* **Assemble a Support Study Partner:** Ask a high-performing coursemate to share photocopy access to their comprehensive notebooks. Study in public campus libraries during daylight hours rather than reading late at night in isolation.
* **Be Gentle with Your Brain:** If you can only study for 25 minutes before panic sets in, celebrate those 25 minutes. Healing from violent trauma is non-linear. Give yourself the grace to recover step by step.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the common symptoms of acute PTSD after a student lodge robbery?
Hypervigilance (jumping at every loud knock or generator rumble), sleep paralysis, recurrent nightmares, inability to read without panic, persistent fear of being alone in your room, and emotional numbness.

### Can a student defer exams or get an extension after a violent campus robbery?
Yes. Obtain an official police incident report and a medical assessment from the university health clinic or counseling unit. Submit these alongside a formal application for exam deferral to your faculty Dean.

### Is it safe to continue living in a lodge after it has been burgled?
If the landlord refuses to reinforce broken iron doors, repair compromised perimeter fences, or hire security guards, staying puts you at acute risk of repeat burglaries. Relocate immediately to a friend's room or compound.

### How can you study when your laptop, phone, and lecture notes were stolen in an attack?
Borrow coursemate notebooks to photocopy immediately. Back up all restored notes on cloud services (Google Drive/Telegram). Visit the university ICT center or main library to access free desktop computers for coursework.
