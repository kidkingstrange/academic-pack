# Legal Squatters: Managing Life in an Overcrowded Room

It is 6:15 AM in a room with four bunk beds and ten occupants.

You wake up on your 2-inch foam mattress laid out on the concrete floor between two metal beds. 

To stand up, you have to carefully step over two sleeping coursemates without kicking their buckets or knocking over the cooking gas cylinder.

You pack your thin foam mattress, roll it tightly into a cylinder, tie it with a nylon rope, and slide it under the bottom bunk. 

You take your toilet bag, grab your water bucket, and head to the communal bathroom before the line of twenty people forms.

This is the daily life of a university squatter.

In Nigerian federal and state institutions, being a squatter is often viewed with social pity or bureaucratic contempt. Yet, economic realities leave hundreds of thousands of students with no alternative.

Here is the truth that successful alumni know: **Your housing status does not determine your degree classification.**

Many First Class scholars, Student Union presidents, and successful software engineers squatted throughout their university degrees. 

What separates those who thrive from those who drop out is a disciplined operational system designed to neutralize the disadvantages of overcrowding.

Here is the master blueprint for surviving—and academically dominating—while living as a squatter.

---

## 1. The "14-Hour Out-of-Room" Protocol

The greatest mistake a squatter can make is trying to study, socialize, and relax inside an overcrowded room.

In a room with eight to twelve students, somebody is always cooking, playing loud music, arguing about European football, hosting visitors, or sleeping. Trying to do deep academic work in that environment will destroy your focus.

Adopt the **"14-Hour Out-of-Room" Rule**:



**The High-Performance Squatter Daily Rhythm:**
* 05:00 AM – 06:30 AM: Wake up, pack sleeping mat, bathe, groom, and eat breakfast — 07:00 AM – 12:00 PM: Faculty lectures and laboratory sessions — 12:00 PM – 05:00 PM: Departmental library, quiet research carrel, or lunch break — 05:00 PM – 09:30 PM: Main university library (Deep study & assignment completion) — 10:00 PM: Return to room strictly to eat, socialize briefly, and sleep



By treating your hostel room strictly as a sleeping bunk, you eliminate 95% of roommate friction, noise distractions, and territorial disputes.

## 3. The Psychology of Value: Turning Hostility into Respect

In many rooms, registered occupants treat squatters with passive-aggressive hostility. You can transform this dynamic entirely through **Strategic Contribution**:

1. **Be the First to Clean:** When the room floor is dusty or trash is overflowing, don't wait for a roster. Sweep it clean and empty the bin.
2. **Handle the Water Logistics:** When the borehole tap flows, fetch two extra buckets for the room. A squatter who supplies water is an indispensable asset that nobody ever wants to evict.
3. **Share Food Seasonings Generously:** When someone is cooking, offering a spoonful of salt, pepper, or tomato paste cements brotherhood and dissolves social hierarchy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you achieve a First Class CGPA while living as a squatter in an overcrowded room?
Yes. Hundreds of First Class graduates squatted through university. The secret is treating the hostel strictly as a place to sleep and bathe, spending 12 to 14 hours daily studying in quiet libraries and classrooms.

### How should a squatter manage personal hygiene in a room with 10 people?
Wake up 45 minutes before registered occupants (around 5:00 AM) to shower and dress before bathroom queues form, and keep all personal toiletries organized inside a portable waterproof dopp kit.

### Where can a squatter store expensive electronics safely?
Never leave laptops or tablets on floor mattresses. Store them in a locked backpack secured to the bed frame with a steel cable lock, or rent a daily study locker at the university main library.

### How do you handle hostile registered roommates who look down on squatters?
Stay calm, polite, and unfailingly helpful. Maintain immaculate personal cleanliness, never borrow their items, and spend minimal daytime hours in the room to eliminate friction.
