Across commercial corridors in Nigerian universities—such as New Market in UNN, SUB complex in UI, Access Bank corridor in UNILAG, or Motion Ground in OAU:

A significant number of physical retail shops, phone accessory stands, boutiques, and printing kiosks are owned and operated by **undergraduates**.

Owning a physical campus store is a fantastic financial accomplishment:
* Daily cash sales from thousands of passing students and staff.
* Reliable monthly income ranging from ₦100,000 to ₦400,000.
* Practical entrepreneurial training that no classroom textbook can teach.

However, when semester examinations arrive, physical retail ownership becomes an **intense operational trap**:
* If you sit in the shop to attend to customers, you cannot study for your 3-unit departmental core courses.
* If you abandon the shop, your daily cash flow vanishes.
* If you leave an irresponsible friend in charge without systems, you return after exams to find missing inventory, unpaid credits, and an empty cash box.

To survive exams with your profits intact and an 'A' grade on your transcript, you must master the art of **operational delegation**.

Here is the step-by-step handover system for student shop owners during university exam blocks.

---

## The 3 Pillars of Secure Retail Delegation

> ### Retail Shop Delegation Architecture
>
> * **1.** The Pre-Exam Inventory Audit (Every item barcoded, tagged, and logged)
> * **2.** The Cashless Payment Mandate (Zero physical cash handled by assistants)
> * **3.** The Incentive Commission Structure (Align assistant motivation with sales)

---

## Pillar 1: The Pre-Exam Stock Audit (Day T-5)

Five days before your first examination paper, spend four hours conducting a complete **Physical Inventory Audit**:
1. Count and record every single physical item on your shelves in a digital spreadsheet (or retail app like Bumpa or Kippa):
   * *"34 iPhone fast-charging cables (₦2,500 retail)."*
   * *"18 packs of A4 printing paper."*
   * *"12 unisex oversized vintage shirts (₦6,000 retail)."*
2. Print or handwrite the finalized stock inventory sheet.
3. Have your temporary shop assistant sign the sheet acknowledging: *"I acknowledge receiving exactly these item counts in good condition on [Date]."*

This single document establishes complete psychological accountability. When assistants know that every single missing item is tracked against their salary, shrinkage drops to zero.

---

## Pillar 2: The Cashless Payment Mandate

The most common way student retailers lose money during delegation is through **unaccounted physical cash transactions**.

Eliminate cash entirely during exam season:
* Place a prominent, laminated printed QR code and bank account standee at the checkout counter:
  > *"PLEASE PAY FOR ALL PURCHASES VIA BANK TRANSFER OR POS TO [BUSINESS ACCOUNT NAME]. NO CASH ACCEPTED."*
* Every single customer transaction goes directly into your verified business bank account on your phone.
* Your assistant never touches paper money; they simply verify that the transaction was confirmed before bagging items.

> ### The Zero-Cash Checkout System
>
> * Customer purchases shirt -> Pays via Bank Transfer/POS to your account ↓ You receive immediate credit alert on phone -> Assistant bags item ↓ Result: 100% of revenue sits safely in your bank; zero cash leakage!

---

## Pillar 3: Commission-Backed Assistant Compensation

Never pay a shop assistant purely on a flat hourly rate. A flat rate encourages them to sit on their phone ignoring customers because their pay is identical whether they make 10 sales or zero sales.

Structure their compensation with a **Base + Commission Model**:
* **Base Stipend:** ₦2,000 per day (covers their transport and daily lunch).
* **Performance Commission:** 5% on all gross sales generated above ₦20,000 per day.

This simple incentive structure turns your temporary assistant into an energetic, attentive salesperson who actively greets shoppers, recommends accessories, and drives revenue while you are sitting in the exam hall.

## The 10-Minute Daily Audit (6:30 PM Protocol)

During exam week, do not hang around your shop during the day.

At 6:30 PM, on your walk back from the library:
1. Stop by the shop for exactly 10 minutes.
2. Match the total daily sales recorded in your POS/ledger against the electronic transfer alerts received on your phone.
3. Check the stock count of the top 3 fastest-moving inventory items.
4. Pay your assistant their daily stipend, thank them warmly, lock the security gates, and return to your room for revision.

---

## Action Checklist: Student Retail Delegation

- [ ] Complete a physical inventory audit and document all stock 5 days before exams.
- [ ] Have your temporary shop assistant physically sign the stock handover sheet.
- [ ] Enforce a 100% cashless transfer and POS checkout policy at the counter.
- [ ] Implement a base stipend plus 5% sales commission compensation structure.
- [ ] Restrict physical shop visits to a single 10-minute audit at 6:30 PM daily.
- [ ] Protect your exam focus—business continues, but your degree takes first priority.
