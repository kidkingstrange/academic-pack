8 Essential Things to Inspect in Off-Campus Lodges

It is 2:30 PM on a scorching Saturday afternoon in a university satellite community—Ekosodin in Benin, Tanke in Ilorin, or Hilltop in Nsukka.

An energetic local estate agent is leading you down an unpaved laterite street. He unlocks the padlock on an empty single-room self-contained apartment:

"Bro, this lodge is pure luxury! Constant borehole water, personal prepaid meter, tiled floor, compound gate locks by 10:00 PM. Graduating final year student just moved out this morning. Five other students are bringing money right now, so if you don't pay ₦350,000 today, it’s gone."

You look at the freshly painted white walls, the shiny porcelain floor tiles, and the clean aluminum sliding window. You feel the pressure. You pull out your phone, open your mobile banking app, and prepare to transfer your rent.

Stop.

Every year, thousands of Nigerian undergraduates sign tenancy agreements for apartments that look immaculate on inspection day, only to move in two weeks later and discover a living nightmare:
* The borehole pump has been broken for three months, forcing occupants to trek 800 meters with 25-liter jerrycans every dawn.
* The "prepaid meter" is actually an unmetered estimated billing line saddled with ₦280,000 in inherited historical debt from former tenants.
* The ceiling board rots and collapses the moment the first heavy torrential rain hits in April.

Before you hand over a single Naira of your family's hard-earned savings, walk through this non-negotiable 8-Point Off-Campus Lodge Inspection Checklist.

1. The 8 Critical Inspection Checkpoints

Lodge Physical Inspection Protocol:
* Utility Infrastructure — Checkpoint 1: The Borehole & Pumping Mechanism (Overhead capacity, tap color) — Checkpoint 2: The Electricity Line & Prepaid Meter Verification — Checkpoint 3: Mobile Network Signal Strength Inside the Room — Structural Integrity & Comfort — Checkpoint 4: The Dry-Season Ceiling Board "Watermark" Test — Checkpoint 5: Cross-Ventilation & Natural Lighting — Checkpoint 6: Bathroom Drainage Pitch & Plumbing Slopes — Security & Legal Boundaries — Checkpoint 7: Perimeter Fencing, Gate Protocols & Burglary Proofing — Checkpoint 8: The "Independent Neighbour Interview" (The Secret Weapon)

Checkpoint 1: The Borehole & Pumping Mechanism

Never ask the caretaker "Is there water?" The caretaker will always answer yes. Instead:
* Walk to the bathroom and open the tap yourself.
* Inspect the color and odor of the water. If it has a brownish tint or smells like rusty iron, the borehole was drilled into an unpurified ferruginous aquifer. Bathing with it will ruin your white shirts and trigger dermatological reactions.
* Ask: "Who operates the generator when NEPA takes light—is fuel contributed collectively, or does the landlord provide diesel?"

Checkpoint 2: The Electricity Line & Prepaid Meter Debt

Check the electricity meter mounted on the wall:
* Is it a dedicated prepaid meter dedicated exclusively to your room, or a shared bulk meter for 16 rooms?
* If it is a shared meter or estimated postpaid line, demand to see the most recent electricity bill. Landlords frequently conceal hundreds of thousands of Naira in accumulated utility arrears, which the electricity distribution company (DisCo) will enforce by disconnecting the entire compound in the middle of your exams.

Checkpoint 3: Mobile Network Signal Strength (The 4G/5G Test)

Step inside the room, shut the door and windows completely, and take out your smartphone:
* Check your cellular data bars for MTN, Airtel, and Glo.
* Run a quick Google speed test.
If your data signal drops from 4G to 'E' (EDGE) or 'No Service' once inside the concrete walls, you will not be able to attend virtual Zoom lectures, submit assignments on the university portal, or download study PDFs without walking out to the street.

Checkpoint 4: The Ceiling Board "Watermark" Test

If you are inspecting during the dry months between November and March, every room looks dry and clean.
* Look directly up at the ceiling boards (asbestos or POP).
* Scan for faint circular brownish-yellow rings or sagging board seams. A watermark indicates that rainwater pools on the roof sheet and leaks straight into the room during torrential downpours.

Checkpoint 5: Cross-Ventilation and Natural Lighting

Hostel rooms in Nigeria without proper cross-ventilation transform into oppressive ovens during blackouts:
* Does the room have at least two windows, or only one small window facing an adjacent concrete wall 12 inches away?
* If electricity goes off at 1:00 PM, does the room receive enough natural daylight to read without straining your eyes?

Checkpoint 6: Bathroom Drainage Pitch and Toilet Flushing

Pour half a bucket of water onto the bathroom floor:
* Does the water flow swiftly toward the drain trap within 60 seconds?
* Or does it pool in stagnant puddles near the door due to poor masonry pitch?
* Flush the toilet bowl to verify adequate water pressure and check for sewer gas back-drafts resulting from missing P-traps.

Checkpoint 7: Perimeter Fencing and Iron Burglary Proofing

Personal safety is non-negotiable:
* Are the window burglary proofing bars made of hollow aluminum or solid reinforced iron rebar welded deep into the lintel?
* Does the compound have a secure perimeter wall with razor wire or broken glass shards on the coping?
* Does the compound gate have an assigned padlock and a clear night curfew?

Checkpoint 8: The Independent Neighbour Interview (The Secret Weapon)

This is the single most valuable step in property inspection.

When the agent steps aside to answer a phone call, walk over to a current tenant living in the lodge or a student in the adjacent compound. Ask them privately:

"Hi, I'm thinking of renting Room 4. How is the landlord with repairs, how often does the borehole pump, and have there been any burglary incidents here this session?"

In two minutes, a fellow student will tell you the brutal truth that no agent or landlord will ever disclose.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why should you never inspect a student lodge only in the dry season?

During the dry season, severe roof leaks, damp rising walls, flooding drainage pathways, and access road mud are completely invisible. If inspecting during dry months, examine ceiling boards for brown watermark rings.

What is the difference between an individual prepaid meter and an estimated bill in student lodges?

An individual prepaid meter (Conlog or Mojec) allows you to pay only for the electricity units you consume. Shared estimated billing frequently leads to fierce roommate feuds over who used electric boilers or hotplates.

Should a student pay caution fees directly to property caretakers or agents?

Caution fees and rent should be paid directly into the verifiable bank account of the registered property owner or recognized legal attorney, accompanied by a signed and dated payment receipt specifying refund terms.

How can you verify if borehole water in a student lodge is drinkable or clean?

Turn on the tap and fill a clear transparent plastic bottle. Inspect for reddish iron sediment, cloudiness, or sulfuric odor. Check whether the compound has overhead filtration tanks or pumps raw ground water.
